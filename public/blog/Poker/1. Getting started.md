# Poker, Poker, Poker

People often perceive poker to be a gambling game. While it is a game of variance (you can play perfectly and still lose, and play awfully and still win), it is still a game of skill and the best players will always win in the long run. Theres a reason why there is even such a thing as professional poker players, players like Daniel Negraneau, Alex Foxen, Jason Koon, have been dominating for years, not just because they're lucky, but because they're just better than everyone else.

The reason why I love playing poker is because I believe it to be an intellectual sport that has more variables and areas for skill expression than other games like Chess, which I think relies more on pure compute and memorization (also i'm just bad at chess). ### What is the objective? 

For me, the goal of poker is to: 
1. Win the most amounts of chips, money, big blinds in a certain timeframe. 

Of course the goal varies if we're playing cash or a tournament format. I often think players are too concerned with making the "correct" decision as opposed to the best decision given the circumstance, and there is a difference between the two and i'll explore that later on. 

### How do we acheive this? 

You win money in poker by either winning at showdown, or if your opponents fold their cards. You use bet sizing to interact with the board and other players and our goal when betting is to: 
1. Get better hands to fold (bluffing) 
2. Get worse hands to call (value betting)

With these two ideas in mind, betting makes a lot more sense if you have an objective to acheive. 

### Choose your game

In poker there are a plethora or game modes and formats. The two main variatns that people play are:

1. Texas Hold'em (2 cards)
2. PLO, Pot Limit Omaha (4 cards)

Texas Hold'em is the most popular variant of poker and is probably the one that most people are familiar with where each player gets 2 hole cards and tries to make the best 5-card hand using both their cards and the community cards. PLO is similar but instead each player gets 4 hole cards, and can only bet the size of the pot. 

The 2 formats are: 

1. Cash 
In cash, players can buy in with a varying amount depending on the stakes, usually 100 big blinds, so if you were playing a $1/$2 game ($1 small blind, $2 big blind), players will usually buy in for a minimum of $200. Players can re-buy or add to their stack at any point in the game (not in the middle of a hand) and the game ends when you decide to leave (cashout). There is some etiquette though, you usually don't want to leave after only playing short-period or right after winnning a large pot, this is called a hit-and-run and is generally frowned upon. 

2. Tournament 
In tournmaent, players will buy in and everyone recieves the same starting stack. The blinds start small and ramp up as the tournament proceeds and prizes are distributed as you place higher in a tournament. It ends when all players are eleminated, and one player is left standing. 

These 2 modes play quite differently. In cash, you can afford to be more patient and play your better hands only since the blind size stays consistent and generally isn't too big of a hit to lose. In tournament, you're incentivized to play more hands since your stack size shrinks significantly as the blinds raise so you need to constantly be building your stack to move on. There are many other variables that affect your playstyle in these formats as well but that can be a separate post.
